The strongest single piece of evidence is CONTEXT.md. It is a 150-line glossary the team had to write to keep the app’s own nouns from colliding. It has an Avoided terms section. It says that “workspace” is “overloaded across local drafts, MLS groups, field sessions, and AI chat.”

When a product needs a glossary to tell an edit state from a workspace from a draft from a conversation, the glossary is the bug report.

The rules in that glossary are right. Never auto-retarget, never auto-publish, never turn a private draft public by navigating. This audit keeps every one of those rules. It removes the number of objects they have to protect.

Internal state leaks onto the screen

  • The Contexts list shows TAXONOMY NONE OPEN CLOSED. Those are raw enum values of contextUse, validationMode and the attachment policy.
  • The chat header shows ctx(model) 262,144, Reasoning (46 lines), Tool Result (61 lines), get_editor_state · 1 tool call(s), and a safety setting labelled Just accept. A developer console is rendered as the primary conversation.
  • The Map Stack offers Sightings layer · suggested and Live beacons layer · suggested on a relay holding six sightings and zero beacons.
  • The top bar reads 3/3 · BROWSE · Public · Unattached · File. Four state machines share one row.
  • On a phone, the largest text on the map is Public · Unattached. “Unattached” is a word from the group-attachment lane of the spec.
Chat cannot be used until you answer a question you didn’t ask. “Choose New map or Use current edit before sending.” A conversation is its own object with a targetWorkspaceId that must be bound first. Four cursors for “what is on screen”

The Map Stack says what is drawn. The Inspector says what is being read. The sidebar list says what is being browsed. The route says what was opened. A Context can set a browse scope that filters lists without touching the map, or sit on the Stack without being inspected. Each is defensible alone. Together they ask the user to hold four cursors in their head.

Earthly today, in one sentence: one person and an AI make an explanatory thematic map and write an essay around it, on a desktop.

That is a real genre and the UI should serve it well. It is also a snapshot of early usage, not a ceiling. Open attachment (other people’s maps joining a shared topic), private circles, sightings and live positions are deliberate use cases that haven’t had their moment yet, and the redesign designs for them explicitly rather than deducing their absence from a 32-map sample. What the sample does justify is ordering: make the map-and-essay path effortless first, and make belonging simple enough that the first person who wants it succeeds.

Names live inside 300 KB blobs. To print a list of titles a client has to download and parse every FeatureCollection. Stories have no title tag either. This shows up as slow lists, empty link previews, and a landing page that can’t show thumbnails.

Five confusions, one root

CONTAINER

Six nouns for one thing

Local draft, workspace, edit state, conversation, Map Stack entry, Inspector subject. A person wants one: this map.

WHERE IT GOES

Three ideas in one pill

Audience (who can read), belonging (what it’s filed under) and filter (what I’m browsing) are orthogonal. “Public · Unattached” is two of them fused, and it changes with the route.

CONTEXT

A poisoned word

Group entity, browse scope, c attachment, the model’s request context, React context. Unrecoverable as a user term. The entity behind it is sound: a place maps belong to, with a pinned lane and an open lane behind a door policy. The confusion is that belonging got fused into the destination pill and a browse scope, so it changed whenever the route did.

AI TARGET

Chat is a peer, not a property

A free-floating conversation must be bound to an edit. That is why “New map / Use current edit” and the BindingChip exist, and why the question “how do I assign what the AI edits?” gets asked at all.

MODE

Six parallel state machines

Stance, view mode, sidebar view, mobile tab, Inspector subject, Stack visibility. Every transition is reconciled against the other five. That is what 5,600 lines of composition root is made of.

THE ROOT

A workbench of panels around a map

Every capability got its own panel, noun and route. The alternative is a map with a margin: one canvas, one panel that always shows exactly one object, and one conversation that belongs to that object.

Shelf, Belongs to and Drafts are places on the screen, not objects: a chip strip, a field on the Map, a list.

Three surfaces

Canvas. The map, full bleed on every device.

Margin. One panel. It shows exactly one object at a time: a Map, a Story, a Place, a Person, or the Shelf. Its back button is browser back. Desktop: a 380px column. Phone: a bottom sheet with three detents.

Thread. The conversation that belongs to the Map in the Margin. A tab of the Margin, not a separate panel. On desktop it can be pulled out to the right as a second column; that is layout, not a different object.

Six nouns

Why a “margin”? An atlas plate has a legend in its margin: the one place that tells you what you are looking at. Earthly’s panel should do exactly that job and no other. The word also gives the team a way to say “that goes in the margin” instead of inventing a seventh panel.

Story37520Writing that puts maps together. Prose first; the maps it references come along.

Story and Atlas are deliberately two nouns. A Story is writing that pulls maps in. An Atlas is a place that maps push themselves into. People understand “I wrote about these maps” and “my map belongs in that atlas” as different acts, and the wire already models them as a versus c.

Two verbs

Open puts an object in the Margin and its geometry on the Canvas. Read-only. Edit turns the Map in the Margin into your working copy, or a fork if it isn’t yours. Exactly one Map is in Edit at a time. It wears a pencil.

That is the whole mode system. There is no browse / focus / author. The URL is the object: /map/naddr is Open, /map/naddr/edit is Edit, ?on= lists what else is on the Shelf.

The Shelf replaces the Map Stack

A chip strip along the bottom of the Canvas. One chip per Map currently drawn. Tap to Open it in the Margin; the eye toggles visibility; × removes it. The chip with the pencil is the Map being edited. Sightings and Live collapse into one Live chip, off by default, with a count when something is nearby or recent.

Where it goes, split into three homes

  • Audience lives on the Publish split button: Publish to Everyone ▾ → Everyone · Circle: Alpine rescue · Nearby: Saturday survey. Stored per working copy. No global pill.
  • Belonging lives in the Map’s details in one field, Belongs to: zero or more Atlases (written as c tags on publish) plus Topics (t hashtags, controlled l labels). Under it, read-only, Appears in lists the Stories that reference the Map. Belonging is a property of the Map, edited where the Map is edited, never shown as global state.
  • Filter lives in Search as a removable chip: Browsing in Roman ruins or #submarine-cables. It only affects lists. Never the Canvas, never what gets published.

Atlases: the open-attach lane, given one home

The lane stays exactly as specified. What changes is that it has one place in the UI and one vocabulary.

  • An Atlas is a page. Opening it fills the Margin like a Story: description, owner, door policy, then two lists, Pinned (the a lane) and Added by others (the c lane, filtered by the door policy). “Show all on map” fills the Shelf. Being in an Atlas sets the Search filter chip and nothing else.
  • Door policy is a sentence, not an enum. open · schema · closed becomes “Who can add maps here: Anyone / Anyone, if the map fits the schema / Only me.” The schema builder sits behind the middle option.
  • Adding is done from either side, always explicitly. From a Map: Belongs to → Add to atlas… (a Publish update if already published). From an Atlas: Add a map → one of mine, or new map in this atlas, which opens a working copy with Belongs to pre-filled. The route suggests belonging for a new working copy only; it never rewrites an existing one.
  • Fit is shown on the Map, where the author can act. Each Belongs-to chip carries a state.
In Roman ruins ✓Roman ruins · doesn’t fit: missing “period”Cool historical maps · closed, ask the owner
  • Accepting is pinning. In a closed or schema atlas the owner accepts a foreign map by pinning it. The a lane already expresses approval, so no new status kind is needed. The owner sees a Waiting list: maps that carry the c tag but aren’t pinned yet.
  • The owner’s AI works on the Atlas. An Atlas has no geometry, so its Thread is a concierge over the collection: “which maps lack descriptions”, “translate the Arabic names across the pinned maps.” It opens the owner’s own maps in Edit one at a time, and for other people’s maps it drafts proposals (kind 37519) that appear as ghosts to the original author. Visible scope at every step.

Every Map has one working copy

  • Open a published Map: you read it. Press Edit: if it’s yours, Earthly creates or resumes your working copy. If not, the button says Fork and the copy is a new Map with lineage.
  • The working copy persists on the device. Navigating away never loses it. The single list of unfinished work is Drafts, under Me and as a badge on the chip. No scratch workspaces, no retained edit states as concepts.
  • Publish update is the default on your own Map (same d, new version). Publish as new map is the secondary item. This fixes the double-publish seen on the relay.
  • Pressing Edit on a second Map asks: “Finish with Hippie Trail first? Keep draft · Discard · Cancel.” Keep is the default.
“Save this view” makes a personal Atlas. Today the stack is URL query state. An Atlas with only pins and “Only me” as the door policy is the shareable Shelf, with no new object type.

Why not fold Atlas into Story? Because push and pull are different acts with different owners. A Story author decides what’s in it; an Atlas lets other people decide to belong. Collapsing them would put the attach lane back inside prose, which is where it got confusing.

Sightings, Live and Circles are unchanged on the wire. Only their place in the UI changes: from destinations to actions and audiences.
4

Talking to a map

This is the answer to two of the questions in the brief: how does the user assign what the AI edits, and how do they know what is being edited and what isn’t.

You don’t assign the AI a target. You open a map and talk to it.

The Thread lives in the Map’s margin

  • There is no unbound chat. The Thread is a tab in the Margin of the Map that is Open. Its header is the Map’s title and state: Hippie Trail · editing or Hippie Trail · read-only. On a read-only Map the send button reads Edit & send and puts the Map into Edit before the first tool call; if it isn’t yours, Fork & send. That is the whole binding step, and it happens in the same gesture as sending.
  • A global Ask exists, but it’s a concierge. With no Map open, the search field takes a question. The answer is read-only: find, measure, explain, geocode. Its only write affordance is a button, Start a map from this, which creates a Map, opens it in Edit, and moves the conversation into its Thread.
  • Scope is selection. Writes go to the Map in Edit, period. If features are selected, the composer shows 12 features selected ×. The model receives the selection; the user sees the scope; clearing the chip widens it to the whole Map.
  • References are drag-in and read-only. Other Maps and Stories can be dragged from the Shelf or Search into the composer as reference chips. The spec already says references never grant edit rights. Keep that sentence.
  • One run at a time, shown where it happens. The pencil chip pulses while the AI works. Navigating elsewhere never cancels the run; the pulsing chip is how you find your way back.
What moves out of the transcript. Reasoning, tool calls, token counts, ctx(model), and per-tool timings go to a Details drawer that is closed by default and remembered per user. The transcript keeps one collapsed line per operation: “Drew 8 corridors and 72 nodes.”

The safety levels stay, renamed from “Just accept” to what they do: Apply automatically · Ask before changing · Ask before every change.

Show the change on the Canvas, not in the transcript

AI-proposed geometry renders as ghosted, dashed, amber features. A diff bar sits on the Canvas. Accepting is a Canvas gesture. Three geometry states and one rule replace the BindingChip, the destination pill, the stance label and the “which draft is active” navigator:

Published

Solid, muted. Someone’s finished map, including your own published version.

Your working copy

Solid, saturated. Its chip on the Shelf wears the pencil. This is what you and the AI edit.

Proposed

Dashed amber ghost. Pending from the AI or from a collaborator’s proposal. Apply or Discard on the Canvas.

Every feature belongs to a chip on the Shelf. The chip with the pencil is what’s being edited. Hover a feature and its chip lights up.

Why this works for the “clean up a convoluted context” story. The user opens the map, selects the ports, says “recolor these and translate the names.” The scope chip says 12 selected, the ghosts show the recolor before it lands, the diff bar says ~12 · +0 · −0. Nothing else on the Canvas can be touched, and the user can see that.

6

The protocol half

The Nostr developer’s rule for the afternoon: change the spec only where the UI above can’t be built honestly without it, and prefer an existing NIP over an Earthly kind so other clients can take part.

Give Maps and Stories tag-level metadata

Add to 37515 and 37520: title, summary, image (a Blossom URL), published_at, following NIP-23 conventions. Add to 37515: features (count) and size (bytes). Lists, cards, link previews and the landing gallery then render without fetching 300 KB blobs. The publisher renders a static PNG thumbnail on publish. This is the single highest-leverage change for discovery on both desktop and phone.

Keep 37518 and both lanes; rename, and make the states legible on the wire

  • Kind 37518 stays as the Atlas. The a pinned lane, the c attach lane and the governance ladder are unchanged. The one legacy-shaped event in the wild is already skipped by the v2 gate.
  • Give the Atlas the same tag-level metadata as Maps and Stories (title, summary, image; bbox is already there) so atlas cards and the landing gallery render without parsing content.
  • Acceptance is expressed by pinning. A foreign map that carries c and also appears in the owner’s a lane is accepted. Clients compute the Waiting list (c present, a absent) with no new kind. Write this into §3.3 so other clients agree.
  • Fit results stay client-side. Schema validation remains validate-on-fetch; verdicts are never published as events. The fit chips are derived.
  • “Save this view” writes a personal Atlas. No NIP-51 set kind is needed.
  • Freeform t and controlled L/l keep their §7 roles and power a Topic search page; they complement c, they don’t replace it.

Comments move to NIP-22 kind 1111

SPEC §2 already uses NIP-22 threading tags on kind 37517. The relay already holds 500 kind-1111 comments from other clients. Adopting 1111 with K=37515 / K=37520 lets any NIP-22 client read and reply to Earthly comments. Reactions stay kind 7; zaps stay 9735. The custom kind buys nothing and costs interoperability.

Lineage, provenance, hygiene

  • Updates. Keep 37515 addressable; make Publish update the default so d is reused; keep v and the previous-version pointer from SPEC §15. Fork writes a new d with a fork-marked a pointer so the original author sees forks under “Appears in”.
  • Honest provenance. The culture is AI-made maps; say so on the wire with ["l","ai-assisted","earthly"], set automatically when a Thread contributed geometry. Readers can filter either way. A Story may get a “How this was made” section generated from the Thread, published only on explicit action.
  • Relay write policy. Accept profiles, Earthly kinds, shelves, and comments / reactions / zaps that reference an Earthly event. Reject the rest at write time. It fixes discovery quality, search relevance, “recent”, and the disk. It is the cheapest change in this document and should ship first.
Unchanged on the wire: 37521 Live, 37522 Sighting, MLS circles, field-session transport, NIP-40 expiry, the L/l · t discipline.

No clean break needed. Nothing above changes an event’s shape; tags are added and a derivation rule is written down. This is a v2.1, not a v3. The one shape change, comments to kind 1111, is a new kind beside the old one.
7

Native Android, or not

Recommendation

Do not build a native Android app now. Re-conceive the mobile shell inside the existing Tauri build as a separate composition root with the four-destination layout above. Set explicit triggers for revisiting native.

  1. The phone’s job is different, not smaller. Authoring is a desktop activity in the relay sample. On a phone people read a shared Story, drop a Sighting, share a live position, look at a saved region on a hike, join a nearby session. None of that needs vertex editing. A responsive collapse of the workbench is the wrong shape; a Kotlin rewrite of that same shape would be the wrong shape in another language.
  2. Native forks four runtimes. The GeoEditor, the applesauce Nostr runtime, the MLS private-workspace runtime, and the AI tool loop with its QuickJS sandbox would all need Android twins, and the safety gates around AI mutation would have to be re-proven. That is the whole project’s risk surface, twice.
  3. What native buys is mostly reachable already. Background location for Live, a foreground service for LAN sharing (built), deep links (built), a SQLite outbox (built), saved regions (built). The remaining gap is MapLibre frame rate in a WebView on low-end devices and gesture fidelity. That is a benchmark, not a belief. Run it on the reference device before deciding.
  4. Seven authors. A second client is a maintenance commitment the community can’t yet justify.

Triggers to revisit

  • Sustained weekly mobile authors above a number the team picks.
  • A measured frame-budget miss for MapLibre on the reference Android device.
  • An OS capability Tauri’s plugin surface cannot reach.
Make Sighting and Live first-class on the phone. The + button, camera attach, “here” as the default location, one-tap share. These are the only content types whose natural home is a pocket, and they currently sit behind two of eighteen tabs.

What this does to the big files. The Margin removes most of AppSidebar and the Inspector; the single router removes the stance / viewMode / mobileTab reconciliation from GeoEditorView; the Thread-in-margin removes the binding branch from the chat store. None of it touches GeoEditor, Authoring or the relay.

What the two of them didn’t settle

  1. Should a closed Atlas show its Waiting maps to visitors, or only to the owner? Discoverability of unaccepted work against the owner’s editorial control.
  2. Should a Thread ever be publishable, or is “How this was made” always a human-edited Story section?
  3. Is one Map in Edit at a time too strict for “compare two maps and merge,” or does Fork-then-reference cover it?
  4. Who renders the thumbnail: the client on publish, or a job the relay operator runs?
  5. What is the reference Android device and frame budget that would flip the native decision?
